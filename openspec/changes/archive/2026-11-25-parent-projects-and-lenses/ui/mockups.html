Design study · outcome parents and lenses · v2

Name the goal. Let lenses show everything else.

A project name was carrying three things: what the work achieves, where it happens (the app and the layer), and who manages it. In this version they come apart. The tree answers who manages what: each parent is named for an outcome, such as Accessible contrast or Light themes, and its children are the deliverables. Tags answer where. Wiffletree works them out from the repositories each project's tickets touch, so no name needs "Chrome" or "backend" in it. Lenses regroup the same sidebar by outcome, by app, by layer, or by what's waiting on you, and nothing gets renamed or moved. The parent and child views, Agents in the right rail, and questions routed through the parent all stay as they were in the first study. New pieces have a dashed outline. Its projects, repositories, tags, counts and states are invented Celadon samples.

Part 2

Where tags come from

Each repository has an app and a layer. You set them once per repository, on the Space's Repositories list, and Wiffletree suggests them when a repository is added. A project's tags are the tags of the repositories its tickets work in, so they update as tickets come and go. You never tag a project by hand.

Naming, in four rules
  • A parent is named after an outcome users or the business care about: Accessible contrast, Light themes, Sponsorships, Onboarding. Never an app (Chrome, Neovim) or a layer (Backend).
  • A child is named after one deliverable, in a few words: "Daylight variant, softer whites", "Generated palette check". Don't repeat the parent's name in it.
  • Platform is the standing parent for work with no product outcome: CI, Terraform, DNS, upgrades. It's marked standing and never shows as ready to close.
  • Apps and layers never go in a name. Tags show them, and they're always correct because they come from the repositories each project's tickets actually touch.
How it behaves
  • Outcome is the only lens that changes anything. Dragging there moves a project under another parent, and Wiffletree asks first. In the other lenses you can't drag: they're read-only views.
  • A project shows up under every app and layer it touches. A project with no tickets yet shows under "not started" until its first ticket picks a repository.
  • First-word groups retire. Parents do their job now. A project with no parent still sits loose in its Space.
  • Spaces stay the top level in every lens. The Me lens leads with what needs you, and lists other Spaces as collapsed rows with their counts.
  • Coordinators follow the same rules. Their instructions carry the rules above. Wiffletree does not check names: a name like "Chrome: …" or "Backend …" is the instructions' job, not code's.
Open questions for you
  • Are the four Celadon parents right? Is Accessible contrast → Contrast warnings correct, or is it its own outcome? Is Sponsorships too small to be a parent, so that Redo sponsor page should sit loose?
  • Is app plus layer enough? Or do you want a third tag, such as a Linear project or a person?
  • Me lens or Needs you page? I've folded the page into the lens. Do you still want a full page for the details, such as answering questions in place?
