How it behaves
  • Dragging always works. With grouping off, you drag projects in one flat list. With it on, you drag projects inside their group, and drag groups and ungrouped projects among each other.
  • Grouping is the Group button above the project list, on to begin with. Turning it on or off changes only how the list is drawn; your order is kept either way.
  • The group is the first word of a project's name. Capitals and a colon or other punctuation after the word do not matter, so “Wiffletree: a” and “wiffletree b” share a group.
  • A group appears only when two or more projects share a first word. Every other project stays an ordinary row with its full name.
  • Inside a group, a row drops the repeated word. The full name still shows on hover and as the conversation's title.
  • Groups look after themselves. You never create, rename or delete one. To move a project to another group, rename the project. A project cannot be dragged out of its group, because its name decides where it belongs.
  • New projects land at the top, as today. Once a project's name puts it in a group, it joins the top of that group.
  • Existing projects keep today's newest-first order until you move them; a group sits where its topmost project is.
  • A collapsed group shows how many projects it holds and the most urgent state inside: needs you, then blocked, then working.
  • Archiving is unchanged. Archived projects reappear in their group, dimmed, when Archived is on.
  • The order is saved with the workspace, so it survives a relaunch. The Group button's state is saved with this app's other view choices, such as appearance.
