Design study · Review levels

How thoroughly a ticket is reviewed

Static study for the review-levels OpenSpec change. Today every review starts every verifier you configured. With this change you define named levels, such as Full and Light, each a set of your verifiers. The coordinator names a level each time it starts a review, guided by a line of text you can rewrite, and Wiffletree requires every verifier in that level to pass. It shows (a) the Review card in Models, (b) editing and adding a level, and what an existing setup becomes, (c) what the coordinator sees and what the app refuses, and (d) the ticket and the publish approval card naming the level that ran. Sample data is simulated.

Nothing you configured changes. Your verifiers, their order, their instructions and both limits are kept exactly. They become one level named Full, marked Full review, that runs all of them, so every review behaves as it did. No second level is added for you; the first time you press Add level, the form is filled in as Light with your first reviewer ticked. A new install starts with two: Full, with every suggested verifier, and Light, a re-check with the first suggested reviewer.

(b) The pencil opens the level’s name and its “Use it” line for editing; the ticks and the kind are always live. Removing a level asks nothing unless it is the last one marked Full review. Removing a verifier takes it out of every level, and is refused if that would leave a level empty. A cycle that already ran keeps the level name it ran under, whatever you rename or remove later.

(c) The text chooses; the app holds four lines. Which level fits a ticket is decided by the “Use it” lines and the coordinator’s instructions, and you can rewrite both. The app does not judge that choice. It enforces only this: a level must be named; every verifier in it must pass; a re-check never stands in for a ticket’s first review, or for a review that ended blocked; and no level may leave out a verifier that still has an open blocking finding. Because of the last two, a pass always rests on a full review, so accepting a ticket and publishing it need no new rule. Within a review nothing changes: a failed round goes back to the implementer and only the failed verifiers of that level check again.

(d) The ticket’s card names the level of every cycle. The publish approval card’s Review row names it too, and after a re-check a second row shows the full review it rests on and the commit that review saw, so you can tell how much has only been re-checked. In an allowed owner’s repository the push still runs without a card after any review that passed, a re-check included, which is what lets a rebase or a conflict fix go out after one reviewer. A review that ended blocked still always asks you, at every level.

  • Settled with the human on October 13, 2026 (see openspec/changes/review-levels/design.md): (1) two kinds of level, Full review and Re-check, with the app refusing a re-check on a ticket whose last review did not pass, and refusing any level that leaves out a verifier with an open blocking finding; (2) an update adds no level beside your migrated Full, and a new install starts with Full and Light; (3) the coordinator must name a level every time, and none is the default; (4) re-checks are not counted against “Full reviews per ticket”.
  • What this does not measure. After a full review has passed, later changes of any size can pass on re-checks alone. The app does not judge how much changed since the full review, and in an allowed owner’s repository that push goes out without a card. Where a card is shown, it names both commits.
  • Not in this change: the PR keeper, the Skills page, the settings repository, and any per-level choice of model. Until the Skills page exists, “when to use each level” lives in the level’s own “Use it” line plus one sentence in the coordinator’s instructions.
  • The verifier named Style, the Docs level and all tickets, commits and pull requests here are examples. A new install’s suggested verifiers are Tests, Correctness and Regressions, as today.
  • This is a static design study, not the GPUI implementation.
